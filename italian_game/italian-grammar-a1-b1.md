# Italian Grammar Checklist: A1 → B1

A consolidated syllabus based on CEFR-aligned Italian L2 course syllabi and cross-checked against exam preparation material for CILS, CELI, and PLIDA.

Each level assumes full command of the level below it.

---

## Level A1 — Contatto

### Sounds and spelling

- [ ] Vowel and consonant sound–letter correspondences
- [ ] Hard vs. soft **c** and **g** (ca/co/cu vs. chi/che vs. ce/ci; ga/go/gu vs. ghi/ghe vs. ge/gi)
- [ ] The silent **h** (ho, hai, ha, hanno)
- [ ] The digraphs **gn**, **gl**, **sc**
- [ ] Elision and the apostrophe (l'amico, un'amica, dov'è)
- [ ] Written stress accents (città, perché, caffè)
- [ ] Capitalization rules

### Nouns

- [ ] Gender and number: -o/-i, -a/-e, -e/-i
- [ ] Nouns with two gender forms (signore/signora, postino/postina)
- [ ] Suppletive gender pairs (uomo/donna, padre/madre, fratello/sorella)
- [ ] Invariable nouns (la città, il bar, la foto, il cinema)

### Articles

- [ ] Definite articles — full paradigm (il, lo, la, l', i, gli, le)
- [ ] Indefinite articles (un, uno, una, un')
- [ ] Choosing between definite and indefinite
- [ ] Articles with professions (fare l'avvocato vs. essere un avvocato)
- [ ] Article use with possessives

### Adjectives

- [ ] Four-ending adjectives (-o/-a/-i/-e)
- [ ] Two-ending adjectives (-e/-i)
- [ ] Invariable adjectives (rosa, blu, viola, chic, snob)
- [ ] Agreement with the noun
- [ ] Bello and buono before the noun
- [ ] Molto/poco/troppo as adjective vs. adverb

### Pronouns and determiners

- [ ] Subject pronouns (io, tu, lui/lei, noi, voi, loro)
- [ ] Address forms: **tu**, **Lei**, **voi**
- [ ] Direct object pronouns (mi, ti, lo, la, ci, vi, li, le)
- [ ] Reflexive pronouns (mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si)
- [ ] Possessive adjectives and pronouns, including article use and omission
- [ ] Demonstratives (questo, quello)
- [ ] Interrogatives (chi, che cosa, quale, quanto, come, dove, quando, perché)
- [ ] Cardinal and ordinal numbers

### Verbs

- [ ] **Presente indicativo** — all three regular conjugations (-are, -ere, -ire)
- [ ] **-isc-** verbs (finire, capire, preferire, pulire)
- [ ] **Essere** and **avere**
- [ ] **C'è / ci sono**
- [ ] High-frequency irregulars: andare, fare, dire, venire, uscire, bere, stare, dare, sapere, salire, rimanere
- [ ] **Verbi riflessivi** and pronominal verbs in the present (mi alzo, mi chiamo)
- [ ] Modal verbs (**verbi servili**): potere, dovere, volere, sapere
- [ ] **Passato prossimo** — auxiliary choice (essere vs. avere), transitivity
- [ ] Past participle formation — regular and high-frequency irregulars
- [ ] Participle agreement with **essere**
- [ ] **Imperfetto** — description and habitual past
- [ ] **Imperativo** — affirmative and negative (tu, voi)
- [ ] **Stare + gerundio** (present progressive)
- [ ] Using the present tense for the near future (domani parto)
- [ ] Piacere — basic constructions (mi piace / mi piacciono)

### Prepositions and adverbs

- [ ] Simple prepositions (di, a, da, in, con, su, per, tra/fra)
- [ ] Articulated prepositions (del, allo, dalla, nei, sugli…)
- [ ] Core semantic functions: possession, place, time, company, means, material, topic
- [ ] Common prepositional phrases of place and time (davanti a, vicino a, prima di, dopo)
- [ ] Adverbs of manner, time, place, quantity
- [ ] Adverbs of affirmation, negation, and doubt (sì, no, forse, quasi, magari)
- [ ] Basic question formation and intonation

## Level A2 — Sopravvivenza

### Sounds and spelling

- [ ] Single vs. double consonants (casa/cassa, pala/palla, cane/canne)
- [ ] Truncation of common nouns (signor, dottor, professor)
- [ ] The courtesy capital in formal writing ("mi chiedo se Lei…")

### Nouns and articles

- [ ] Irregular masculines in **-a** (il problema, il tema, il programma, il barista)
- [ ] Irregular feminines in **-o** (la mano, la radio, la moto)
- [ ] Irregular plurals (uomo/uomini, uovo/uova, braccio/braccia, dito/dita)
- [ ] Gender of loanwords and their articles (il computer, la password, la toilette)
- [ ] Articles with geographic names (rivers, seas, regions, countries, mountain ranges)
- [ ] Article omission with cities and small islands
- [ ] Article omission before singular family possessives (mio fratello vs. i miei fratelli)
- [ ] Exceptions: mamma, papà, and altered or modified family nouns
- [ ] **Articolo partitivo** — singular and plural (del pane, delle mele)
- [ ] Partitive alternatives: un po' di, alcuni, qualche, di + article
- [ ] **Tutto + article** (tutto il giorno, tutti i miei libri)
- [ ] Article use and omission with time expressions (alle due, a gennaio, in estate, nel 1840)

### Adjectives

- [ ] **Comparativo di maggioranza / minoranza** — più/meno… di vs. più/meno… che
- [ ] **Comparativo di uguaglianza** (come, quanto, così… come, tanto… quanto)
- [ ] **Superlativo assoluto** — molto + adjective, and the **-issimo** suffix
- [ ] Adjective position and meaning shift (un vecchio amico vs. un amico vecchio; un grande uomo vs. un uomo grande)

### Pronouns

- [ ] Unstressed direct object pronouns — placement in the sentence
- [ ] Indirect object pronouns — unstressed and stressed forms (mi / a me, gli / a lui)
- [ ] Past participle agreement with direct object pronouns in **avere** compounds (l'ho vista, li ho mangiati)
- [ ] **Ci locativo** (ci vado, ci sono stato)
- [ ] Pronoun placement with modal + infinitive (ti posso parlare / posso parlarti)
- [ ] Pronoun attachment to the imperative (dammi, prendilo, non farlo)
- [ ] Relative pronoun **che**
- [ ] Indefinite pronouns: qualcuno, qualcosa, nessuno, niente
- [ ] Ne — first exposure, quantity use (ne voglio due)

### Verbs

- [ ] **Passato prossimo vs. imperfetto** — the central A2 distinction
- [ ] Reflexive and reciprocal verbs in the passato prossimo (essere + agreement)
- [ ] Modal verbs in compound tenses — auxiliary choice (ho dovuto / sono dovuto andare)
- [ ] **Futuro semplice** — regular and high-frequency irregular forms
- [ ] Future for predictions, plans, promises, and probability (Saranno le tre)
- [ ] **Condizionale presente** — forms and uses: polite requests, wishes, doubt
- [ ] Modals in the conditional (vorrei, potrei, dovrei)
- [ ] Sapere vs. conoscere; independent uses of volere, dovere, potere
- [ ] Andare vs. venire; portare vs. prendere
- [ ] **Imperativo formale (Lei)** — introduction
- [ ] Impersonal **si** — introduction (in Italia si mangia bene)

### Prepositions, adverbs, connectives

- [ ] Deeper preposition use for place, time, material, and cause
- [ ] Expanded prepositional phrases (come, secondo, attraverso, durante, oltre, circa)
- [ ] Prepositions with two possible constructions (dentro il/al mobile, fuori la/della città)
- [ ] Fixed prepositional expressions (d'accordo, per cento, in effetti, per esempio, senz'altro)
- [ ] Expanded adverbs and adverbial phrases (così, ancora, un pochino, già, proprio, davvero, non… più, magari)
- [ ] Focusing adverbs (anche, pure, solo, neanche, nemmeno, neppure)
- [ ] Adverbs in **-mente** derived from adjectives
- [ ] Conjunctions (e/ed, ma, però, tuttavia, perché, quindi, mentre)
- [ ] Narrative time markers (poi, ieri, poco fa, un anno fa, di solito, allora, quando)

## Level B1 — Soglia

### Verbs — the core of the level

- [ ] **Imperfetto** — expanded uses: descriptions, habits, dream narration, journalistic style, polite requests, unrealized past conditions
- [ ] **Passato prossimo** — consolidation: auxiliaries, participle agreement, irregular participles, adverb insertion (ho **appena** chiamato Luca)
- [ ] **Futuro semplice** and **futuro anteriore** — forms and uses
- [ ] **Trapassato prossimo** (ero andato, avevo mangiato)
- [ ] **Condizionale presente** — full range of uses
- [ ] **Condizionale passato** — including future-in-the-past (ha detto che sarebbe venuto)
- [ ] **Congiuntivo presente** — forms and triggers (penso che, credo che, è possibile che, benché, affinché, sebbene)
- [ ] **Congiuntivo passato**
- [ ] **Congiuntivo imperfetto** — introduced here, consolidated at B2
- [ ] Congiuntivo esortativo (venga pure, si accomodi)
- [ ] **Periodo ipotetico** type 1 — reality (se piove, resto a casa)
- [ ] **Periodo ipotetico** type 2 — possibility (se avessi tempo, andrei) *(type 3 is B2)*
- [ ] **Imperativo formale (Lei)** — full command, including with pronouns
- [ ] **Forma passiva** with essere and venire — basic forms
- [ ] **Si impersonale** and **si passivante** (si dice che, si vendono case)
- [ ] **Gerundio** — simple and with stare; basic implicit clauses
- [ ] Aspectual verbs: cominciare a, iniziare a, stare per, smettere di, finire di, continuare a
- [ ] Verb + preposition patterns (cercare di, riuscire a, abituarsi a, accorgersi di, accettare di, affidarsi a)
- [ ] Reported speech — basic, with present and past reporting verbs

### Pronouns and particles

- [ ] **Pronomi combinati** (me lo, te la, glielo, ce ne, gliene)
- [ ] **Ci** — locative and idiomatic (ci penso, ci vuole, ce l'ho, ci credo)
- [ ] **Ne** — partitive, pronominal, and idiomatic; ne vs. lo
- [ ] Relative pronouns: **che**, **cui**, **il quale**, **chi**
- [ ] Stressed pronouns (a me, con lui, secondo noi)
- [ ] Pronoun placement with infinitives, imperatives, and gerunds
- [ ] Pronominal verbs (andarsene, farcela, cavarsela)

### Articles, adjectives, determiners

- [ ] Article omission with indefinites (qualche giorno, alcuni amici) and demonstratives
- [ ] Article use and function with time expressions
- [ ] Indefinite adjectives and pronouns: qualche, alcuni, certo, ciascuno, ogni, qualcuno, qualcosa, altro, nessuno
- [ ] Full comparative and superlative system
- [ ] **Superlativo relativo** (il più bello della classe)
- [ ] Irregular comparatives: migliore, peggiore, maggiore, minore; meglio/peggio

### Prepositions, adverbs, connectives

- [ ] Prepositions with aspectual and high-frequency verbs
- [ ] Prepositional phrases: a causa di, invece di, prima di, dopo aver + participle
- [ ] Adverbs: ora, mai, già, ormai, talvolta, a poco a poco, all'improvviso, tardi, più tardi, presto
- [ ] Compound adverbs (soprattutto)
- [ ] **Connettivi testuali**: anche, inoltre, in più, pure, invece, però, cioè, per esempio, infatti, così, allora, perciò, quindi, dunque, insomma

## Notes on how these levels are actually assessed

Certification exams at all three levels weight **communicative effectiveness over grammatical accuracy**. The CEFR descriptors for A1, A2, and B1 explicitly state that error-free production is not required — what matters is whether the message gets across. Speaking fluently with errors scores better than halting precision.

Three structural pressure points across the whole A1–B1 span:

1. **Passato prossimo vs. imperfetto** — the single largest source of persistent error for English speakers.
2. **The pronoun system** — direct, indirect, combined, plus ci and ne. This compounds across all three levels and is rarely fully consolidated at B1.
3. **The congiuntivo** — the dividing line into B1. Recognition and reliable production in high-frequency triggers is expected; perfection is not.
